The fix is pretty simple

  1. You can use AI. It's a nice tool. It's there to be used, but for the love of our collective sanity, READ what the model said. All of it. Yes, even the lists, the code, the whole thing...
  2. Decide what's useful and what isn't. Models are confident in ways that have nothing to do with being right. Half of it is probably wrong, generic, or both. That's when it isn't a straight up hallucination.
  3. Write your own answer. Three sentences of yours beat three paragraphs of slop every time. Nobody has ever said "Damnnnn boy... I wish that reply had been longer and more robotic".
  4. If you genuinely need to quote the model, fine, there's good stuff in there! Just explain why. "I asked Claude and this part actually checks out:", that's ok! At least you read what happened there, we still like you
  5. If you have nothing to add, say nothing. Silence is a contribution too. Or say "I have nothing to add". That'll save everyone's time.

"But I want to help!"

No, you don't. You're trying to look helpful without doing the work of being helpful. There's a difference there.

Helping is reading carefully, thinking, and replying with something only you could have said, or at least polished. Pasting stuff from an AI tells the person their question wasn't worth your time, only a chatbot's. How would you feel?

This site is not anti-AI.

Use the tools. Use them every day. Use them to draft faster, be more creative, learn more, unblock yourself. Great. That's what they exist for. The output is a starting point, not a deliverable. Treat it like a junior intern's first draft, because that's exactly what it is. Edit it. Cut it. Disagree with it. Make it yours, or don't send it.
